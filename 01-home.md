## 01. Why Water Damage in Haltom City Needs Quick Action

Most homes in Haltom City were built between the 1950s and 1980s. Many still have original galvanized or copper supply lines, older water heaters and slab foundations sitting on North Texas clay. That clay swells and shrinks with the weather, which puts stress on pipes under and inside the slab.

Add in heavy spring storms, hail that cracks roofs and the kind of hard freezes the area saw in 2021, and water damage here is not rare. Properties near Big Fossil Creek and Little Fossil Creek also see runoff during flash flooding.

When it happens, the first few hours decide how big the repair bill gets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How fast can you get to my property in Haltom City?**
In most cases we arrive within 60 minutes of your call, any time of day.

**Will my homeowners insurance cover water damage?**
Sudden and accidental damage, like a burst pipe or a failed water heater, is usually covered. Slow leaks and flood water often are not. We can help you understand your policy and document the damage for your claim.

**How long does drying take?**
Most properties dry in three to five days. Heavily soaked materials or water under flooring can take a bit longer.

**Can I stay in my home during the work?**
Usually yes. If sewage or heavy mold is involved, we may suggest staying out of the affected area until it is cleaned.

**What should I do before you arrive?**
Shut off the water at the main valve if you can, turn off power to wet areas if it is safe, and move valuables off the floor. Then call us.

**Do you charge for the inspection?**
No. The on site assessment is free.
