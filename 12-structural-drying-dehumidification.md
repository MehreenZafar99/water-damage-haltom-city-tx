## 01. Surface Dry Is Not the Same as Dry

Water soaks into porous materials like drywall, wood studs, insulation and concrete. A box fan and an open window will not reach it. Worse, North Texas humidity during spring and summer can slow natural drying even more.

Professional drying controls the air, temperature and airflow in the room so moisture leaves the materials quickly and evenly.

---

## 02. The Science in Plain Words

**Air movers** blow across wet surfaces to push moisture into the air.
**Dehumidifiers** pull that moisture out of the air so it does not settle back in.
**Heat** speeds up evaporation when used correctly.

Balance matters. Too much air without enough dehumidification just moves wet air around. We calculate how much equipment each room needs.

## 04. Our Drying Process

**Timeline Strip**

1. **Measure.** Readings from wet and dry areas give us a target.
2. **Plan.** We calculate the number of dehumidifiers and air movers.
3. **Open up if needed.** Small holes behind baseboards let air into wall cavities.
4. **Run equipment** around the clock.
5. **Check daily.** We log readings and adjust equipment.
6. **Confirm dry.** Equipment leaves only when readings match the dry standard.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long will the equipment run?**
Usually three to five days. Dense materials like hardwood and concrete can take longer.

**Will it raise my power bill?**
Yes, a little. Many insurance policies reimburse the extra electricity cost, and we can provide an estimate for your claim.

**Do you have to cut my walls open?**
Not always. We try to dry in place first. Small drill holes are often enough to reach wall cavities.

**Can I turn the machines off to sleep?**
Please keep them on. Turning them off slows progress and can let mold start.
