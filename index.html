First steps

What is happening at your property right now?

Pick what you see to get the first steps to take before our crew arrives.

A pipe just burst

Hundreds of gallons can come out in an hour. Act fast:

  1. Shut off the main valve at the meter box or in the garage.
  2. Turn off breakers for wet rooms if you can reach them without standing in water.
  3. Open a faucet to drain what is left in the lines.
  4. Move rugs and electronics off the floor.

Water is coming through the ceiling

This is often a roof leak, an attic AC line or an attic water heater.

  1. Keep everyone out from under any sagging area.
  2. Put a bucket under drips and cover furniture.
  3. Turn off the AC if it runs through the attic.
  4. Do not poke a bulging ceiling yourself.

Storm or creek water got inside

Outside flood water carries soil and bacteria, so it needs more than drying.

  1. Stay out of the water and avoid touching it without boots and gloves.
  2. Do not use electricity in flooded rooms.
  3. Take photos of the water line for insurance.
  4. Keep children and pets away.

Sewage is backing up

This is contaminated water. Do not clean it up with a mop and bleach.

  1. Stop flushing toilets and running water in the home.
  2. Keep people and pets out of the area.
  3. Turn off the HVAC if it pulls air from that room.
  4. Throw away any food the water touched.

Floors are wet and you are not sure why

In Haltom City this is often a slab leak, an appliance leak or rain water at the foundation.

  1. Check the dishwasher, washer, water heater and fridge line.
  2. Feel for warm spots on the floor, a sign of a hot water slab leak.
  3. Turn off all water and watch the meter for 30 minutes.
  4. Pull up wet rugs so the floor can breathe.
Call (817) 482-3916

Thermal cameras and moisture meters show where the water is coming from.

There is a musty smell or dark spots

That usually means hidden moisture, and possibly mold.

  1. Do not scrub or spray large patches of mold.
  2. Look under sinks, around the AC and along exterior walls.
  3. Run a fan or dehumidifier in the room for now.
  4. Note when the smell is worst.

Before you call your insurer

Will insurance pay for it?

Usually covered

  • Burst or frozen pipes
  • Water heater or washer failure
  • A broken supply line under a sink
  • Roof leaks caused by a storm

Often not covered

  • Slow leaks that went on for months
  • Outside flood water without flood insurance
  • Damage from lack of maintenance

What we do for your claim

  • Photos and daily moisture logs
  • Itemized estimate insurers accept
  • Meet your adjuster on site
  • Direct billing with most carriers

How fast can you get here?

In most cases a crew arrives within 60 minutes anywhere in Haltom City, day or night.

Do I call my insurance company first?

No. Stop the water and call us so drying starts right away. Then open your claim. Waiting can make the damage worse and weaken your claim.

How long will the drying take?

Most properties dry in three to five days. Concrete slabs and hardwood can take a little longer.

Can I stay in my home?

Usually yes. If sewage or heavy mold is involved, we may ask you to stay out of that area until it is cleaned.

Is the inspection free?

Yes. The on site assessment costs nothing.

Do you also do the repairs?

Yes. Once everything is dry we replace drywall, flooring, texture, paint and trim.
